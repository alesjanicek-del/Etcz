01

Question 1: Look at the email shown. Which details suggest that it is probably phishing? Multiple answers

  1. AThe sender’s address is from a domain the organisation does not use.
  2. BThe email is written in English.
  3. CThe message asks you to enter your password via a link in the email.
  4. DThe message does not address anyone by name.
  5. EThe message puts pressure on you and sets a short deadline.
  6. FThe email has a subject line.
  7. GThe link actually leads to a different domain from the one the company portal uses.

02

Question 2: An email arrives in your work inbox that looks suspicious: an unexpected attachment and a request to click a link. What is the right thing to do?

  1. AI forward the email to everyone in my department to warn them.
  2. BI do not open the attachment or the link, and I report the email following the organisation’s internal procedure.
  3. CI delete the email permanently and tell no one. That settles it.
  4. DI reply to the sender and ask whether they really sent the message.
  5. EI open the attachment only on my work computer, because the company antivirus will catch any malicious code there anyway.

Question 3: When you hover over a link, the target address https://firma.example.overeni-uctu.example/log­in appears. Who actually controls the website the link leads to?

  1. AYou cannot tell until you open the page.
  2. BThe owner of the domain overeni-uctu.example.
  3. CThe owner of the domain firma.example.
  4. DBoth domains together, because both appear in the address.

04

Question 4: You clicked a link in an email and entered your work sign-in details on the page that opened. Only then did you notice that the address does not belong to your organisation. What do you do? Multiple answers

  1. AI report it straight away following the internal procedure, even if nothing obvious has happened yet.
  2. BI wait to see whether any problems appear and only deal with it then.
  3. CI describe exactly what I entered and when, so the security team can check the account.
  4. DI do nothing, because I have multi-factor authentication turned on.
  5. EI permanently delete the email from my inbox and from the deleted items, so no one else clicks it.
  6. FI change my password as soon as possible through the official sign-in page (not the link in the message), or as IT instructs.

05

Question 5: You work in accounts. An email arrives that seems to come from a regular supplier. It announces a change of bank account and asks for the due invoice to be paid to the new account. What do you do? Multiple answers

  1. AI follow the organisation’s approval process for changing payment details (e.g. a check by a second person).
  2. BI report the suspicious request following the internal procedure.
  3. CI reply to the email and ask for written confirmation of the change.
  4. DI call the phone number given in the signature of this email.
  5. EI check the change by phoning the supplier on a number from an earlier contract or our records, not from this email.
  6. FI send the payment, because the logo and the invoice format match earlier ones.

Summary

At a glance

  • You (the organisation) are the controller and we are the processor. We process data only on your documented instructions (Art. 4).
  • We store and process data in the EU or EEA; the list of sub-processors is in Annex 2 (Art. 8).
  • You set the retention period for results; after it, the application irreversibly anonymises the personal data of test takers (Art. 9.1).
  • When the agreement ends, we hand over an export to you and erase the data within 60 days at the latest (Art. 9.4).
  • A support technician enters your organisation only with your consent (Art. 7).

The summary is for orientation only and does not replace the text of the document. The full text below is binding.

1. Parties and relationship to the Main Contract

Controller: the customer of the exam-test service (the organisation that ordered the service, registered for it or concluded a contract with the provider).

Processor: ABUCO ICT s.r.o., with its registered office at Třebohostická 987/5, 100 00 Praha 10, Company ID No. (IČO) 29153905, registered in the Commercial Register maintained by the Municipal Court in Prague, Section C, Insert 204821.

1.1 This agreement governs the processing of personal data that the processor carries out for the controller when providing the service under the Terms of Service or an individual contract (the “Main Contract”). This agreement is concluded together with the Main Contract.

1.2 In matters of personal data protection, this agreement takes precedence over the Main Contract. If the controller concludes its own data processing agreement with the processor, that agreement takes precedence.

2. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of processing

2.1 The processor processes personal data for the controller solely for the purpose of providing the exam-test service: maintaining user accounts, creating and assigning tests, conducting and evaluating exams, issuing and verifying result reports, retaining results, technical support and backup.

2.2 Nature of processing: storage, structuring, retrieval, display, evaluation, generation of a result report, sending of emails, backup, anonymisation and erasure, all by automated means in the application.

2.3 Processing lasts for the term of the Main Contract and thereafter for the time needed to hand over and erase the data under Art. 9. The archive of verification data under Art. 9.5 does not contain names or identifiers of persons.

3. Categories of data subjects and personal data

  • Users of the controller (administrators, instructors, authors, students with an account): name, email, role, branch, sign-in credentials (the password only as an irreversible hash, the two-step verification secret in encrypted form), records of sign-ins and changes (change log).
  • Test takers: name or identifier (if required by the controller), email (if entered, e.g. for sending an access code), answers, results, result reports with a verification code, technical log of the attempt (event times, IP address, browser type); for a test for which the controller has switched on exam monitoring, also monitoring events (leaving the window, exiting full screen, attempting to copy or paste, unusually fast answers).
  • Contact persons of the controller and persons who submit a support request or problem report: name, email, telephone, request text.

3.1 The service does not require special categories of personal data (Article 9 GDPR) or data relating to criminal convictions (Article 10 GDPR). The controller does not enter such data into the application (e.g. into question text, notes or identifiers); if it does, it is responsible for the existence of a legal basis.

4. Controller’s instructions

4.1 The processor processes personal data only on documented instructions from the controller. The instructions are the Main Contract, this agreement, the settings made by the controller in the application (e.g. the retention period for results, protection of a record from anonymisation, anonymisation on instruction, consent to support access, exam monitoring settings) and written instructions from the controller’s authorised persons.

4.2 The same applies to transfers of personal data to a third country or an international organisation. Where Union or Member State law requires the processor to process data, the processor informs the controller before processing, unless that law prohibits it.

4.3 Exam monitoring is switched off by default. By switching it on for a test, the controller determines that monitoring events are to be recorded and, where applicable, that test takers are to be warned or the attempt is to be submitted automatically after a set number of times the window has been left. Before switching it on, the controller assesses necessity and proportionality (in particular for employees and persons with disabilities), the need for a data protection impact assessment (Article 35 GDPR) and, for automatic submission, Article 22 GDPR; it ensures human review at the test taker’s request. Monitoring does not use a camera, microphone, screen recording, biometrics, device fingerprinting or artificial intelligence; it does not evaluate events or infer cheating from them. The processor provides the controller with a description of the function and cooperation under Art. 5.

4.4 If the processor considers that an instruction infringes the GDPR or other data protection law, it informs the controller immediately and need not carry out the instruction until it has been clarified.

5. Processor’s obligations

  • to ensure that persons authorised to process personal data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under a statutory obligation of confidentiality; access is limited to designated staff of the processor to the extent necessary;
  • to take the technical and organisational measures under Article 32 GDPR described in Annex 1; to test the measures regularly and not to reduce the level of protection by changing them;
  • to assist the controller in responding to requests from data subjects (Articles 12 to 22 GDPR), in particular through tools in the application: export of a person’s data (Articles 15 and 20), anonymisation on instruction (Article 17), protection of a record from anonymisation (Article 18); to forward a request received directly from a data subject to the controller without undue delay and not to handle it itself;
  • to assist the controller in fulfilling its obligations under Articles 32 to 36 GDPR (security, breach notification, impact assessment, prior consultation), taking into account the nature of processing and the information available to the processor;
  • to maintain records of processing activities under Article 30(2) GDPR;
  • to make available to the controller the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with its obligations and to allow for audits or inspections conducted by the controller or an auditor mandated by the controller, with at least 15 working days’ prior notice, during working hours and without compromising the security and data of other customers; the controller bears the costs of the audit unless the audit reveals a material breach of this agreement.

6. Personal data breach

6.1 The processor notifies the controller of a personal data breach without undue delay after becoming aware of it, and no later than within 24 hours, by email to the controller’s administrators and to the data protection officer whom the controller states in the organisation contacts in the application.

6.2 The notification contains at least a description of the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed and a contact point for further information. The processor provides information that is not immediately known on an ongoing basis.

6.3 Notification to the supervisory authority (within 72 hours, Article 33 GDPR) and communication to data subjects (Article 34 GDPR) are carried out by the controller; the processor provides cooperation for this. The processor keeps a record of breaches.

7. Support access

The processor’s support technician enters the controller’s organisation in the application only with the controller’s consent set in the application (permanently or for a time window), stating the reason; each entry is recorded in the change log and notified to the controller’s administrators by email. During such an entry, the technician cannot export data and does not open the details of test takers’ results or the details of questions in official tests.

8. Sub-processors and place of processing

8.1 The controller gives a general authorisation to engage sub-processors. The current list is in Annex 2.

8.2 The processor informs the controller of any intended addition or replacement of a sub-processor at least 30 days in advance (by email to administrators and by updating the list). Within this period, the controller may object on legitimate grounds; if no agreement is reached, it may terminate the Main Contract without penalty with effect from the date on which the change would take effect; the processor refunds the proportionate part of the price paid for the period after the end of the contract.

8.3 The processor imposes on each sub-processor, by contract, the same data protection obligations as it has under this agreement, in particular sufficient guarantees of technical and organisational measures. The processor remains liable to the controller for the performance of the sub-processor’s obligations as if it performed them itself (Article 28(4) GDPR).

8.4 Personal data are stored and processed in the European Union or the European Economic Area. If a sub-processor is part of a group based outside the EEA, or if a transfer to a third country may occur, this may happen only with safeguards under Chapter V GDPR (a Commission adequacy decision, e.g. the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, or standard contractual clauses); the processor states this in the list of sub-processors.

9. Retention period, anonymisation and end of processing

9.1 The retention period for exam results is determined by the controller in the organisation settings. When it expires, the application automatically anonymises the personal data of test takers: it removes the name, the identifier and its cryptographic salt, the link to the account, the label and email of the access code, the IP addresses and browser type in the attempt log, and the stored PDF result report bearing the name. Anonymisation is irreversible. The exception is records that the controller expressly protects from anonymisation (record protection, e.g. for ongoing proceedings).

9.2 The application anonymises or deletes other personal data after the following periods, unless the controller agrees otherwise with the processor: unused access codes 12 months after the test assignment ends; names in the waiting room of a supervised in-room exam that the participant did not start, 1 month after the last activity; contact details and text of resolved support requests 24 months after resolution; change log records in the organisation 60 months. Data exports are removed from storage 30 days after they are created. Backups are kept for 30 days; anonymised or erased data therefore cease to exist in them no later than 30 days after anonymisation. When restoring data from a backup, the processor repeats any anonymisation carried out previously.

9.3 The linked attempt records that ensure the evidential value of results, and public verification of result reports, remain functional after anonymisation; they contain only cryptographic hashes and the verification code, from which the person cannot be identified. When verifying with a code, the page shows the person’s initials, and only until anonymisation.

9.4 After the end of the Main Contract, the processor, at the controller’s choice, hands over to the controller an export of all the organisation’s data in a machine-readable format (results, result reports, questions and images, version history, test definitions, attempt logs, change log and data for verifying result reports) and then erases the personal data, including copies, within 60 days at the latest, unless a legal regulation requires their retention. The data are removed from backups upon expiry of the backup retention period (30 days).

9.5 So that issued result reports remain verifiable even after the end of the agreement, the processor, on the controller’s instruction given by this agreement, retains an archive of verification data without names and identifiers of persons: the verification code of the result report, cryptographic hashes of the record and of the records linked to it, time stamps, the name of the organisation and the test, the date and result of the exam and, for certificates, the end of their validity; the archive does not contain exam monitoring events (they are only in the cryptographic hash of the record). The archive is retained for 5 years from the end of the agreement. On the controller’s instruction, the processor erases the archive earlier or removes the exam result from it.

9.6 After the period under Art. 9.5 expires, the processor erases the archive and the result report can no longer be verified on the processor’s verification page. The export under Art. 9.4 contains verification data (linked attempt records and time stamps) from which the controller can demonstrate the authenticity and integrity of result reports even afterwards; the controller should therefore download the export before the end of the Main Contract.

10. Liability and final provisions

10.1 The liability of the parties for harm caused by a breach of obligations in the processing of personal data is governed by Article 82 GDPR; otherwise liability is governed by the Main Contract.

10.2 This agreement remains in force for as long as the processor processes personal data for the controller. The processor announces changes to the template in the same way as changes to the Terms of Service.
